# Business Canvases & Deep-Structure Checklists

**When to use:** Referenced by mu-mrd's Revenue & opportunity section when the revenue story needs more structure than "who pays, why, roughly how much" — canvas-level mapping, unit economics, or naming work. Walk the relevant checklist, fold the conclusions back into the MRD section; the canvas itself stays a working tool, not an artifact section. (Absorbed from the retired mu-biz full mode.)

## Business Model Canvas (9 blocks)

Answer each in one or two lines; a block you cannot fill is a fork to put to the user.

1. **Customer Segments** — who are we creating value for? Which segment pays?
2. **Value Propositions** — what problem does each segment pay to solve?
3. **Channels** — how does the product reach and stay with each segment?
4. **Customer Relationships** — self-serve, assisted, community? Acquisition vs retention?
5. **Revenue Streams** — what does each segment pay for, how (subscription / usage / one-off), at what anchor price?
6. **Key Resources** — what must we own or control (data, infra, brand, expertise)?
7. **Key Activities** — what must we keep doing well (build, curate, support)?
8. **Key Partners** — who supplies what we don't build?
9. **Cost Structure** — biggest cost drivers; fixed vs variable; which scale with revenue?

## Value Proposition Canvas

Pair the two sides; a pain with no reliever (or a reliever with no pain) is scope to cut or a gap to fill.

- **Customer side:** jobs-to-be-done / pains / gains
- **Product side:** pain relievers / gain creators — each mapped to a named pain or gain

## Unit Economics Outline

Only when pricing decisions block MRD approval:

- Revenue per unit (per seat / per account / per transaction)
- Direct cost per unit (infra, LLM tokens, support minutes)
- Contribution margin; break-even volume at anchor price
- The one assumption the model is most sensitive to — name it and flag it as an MRD open question

## Naming & Brand Checklist

Optional; skip unless the product is about to face users/investors:

- Name says what it does or what it evokes — never both badly
- Domain / package / handle availability checked
- Collision scan: trademark-adjacent products in the same space
- Passes the "say it on a phone call" test (spellable, pronounceable)
